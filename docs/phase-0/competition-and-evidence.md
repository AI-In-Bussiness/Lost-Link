## Research work to start before building

Working hypotheses, all currently unverified:

- Students struggle to find an item when staff describe it differently.
- Staff need useful ownership evidence without exposing identifying details publicly.
- A school office could maintain the inventory and keep the service online.

Proposed small discovery sample: 3–5 students and 1–2 school-office staff, subject to school rules and appropriate consent. Ask about the last actual lost-item incident, steps taken, time/effort, outcome, current tools, what information is safe to share, and whether an inventory workflow is feasible. Avoid leading questions such as “Would you use our amazing AI app?” Record no unnecessary names or sensitive student details. These counts are a research plan, not completed interviews. Team members conduct outreach; no messages have been sent.

Compare at least three alternatives: current school office/bin process and two real digital products. Capture source URL/date, target user, workflow, published price if applicable and limitations. Do not assert no competitors or invent product facts. Use findings to reconsider scope if search is not the real bottleneck.

| Evidence ID | Claim/hypothesis | Method / source | Observed result | Limitation | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E-01 | Discovery is difficult for students | Student incident interviews | None yet | Small convenience sample anticipated | Not started |
| E-02 | Staff can maintain and verify inventory | Office workflow interviews | None yet | Local process may not generalize | Not started |
| E-03 | Differentiation versus alternatives | Current process + two sourced product comparisons | None yet | Marketing claims need qualification | Not started |
| E-04 | Language matching improves retrieval | Fixed queries vs keyword baseline | None yet | Fictional data shows capability, not real demand | Phase 4 |
| E-05 | Workflow improves task effort | Timed discovery/claim tasks after prototype | None yet | Usability test cannot establish real recovery rate | Optional validation |

Impact chain: staff lists an item → student discovers it → staff verifies evidence → staff returns it. Candidate measures: search task completion, time to identify a relevant listing, staff steps per review and verified returns. Establish a baseline before claiming improvement. Sustainability proposal: a named school-office owner maintains inventory; school-approved hosting, access review, retention and cost limits are required before real use. No school partnership is currently established.
